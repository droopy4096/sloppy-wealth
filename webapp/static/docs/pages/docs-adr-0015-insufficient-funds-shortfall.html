ADR-0015: Debits are capped at available funds, and the unpaid part is a shortfall

  • Status: Accepted
  • Date: 2026-11-04

Context

In a drawdown scenario (e.g. pension withdrawals in retirement), a cash flow can need more than its source account holds. The engine has to define what happens then.

Decision

  • A debit from an account is capped at the funds available in that period. The account balance never goes below zero.
  • The unpaid remainder is recorded as a shortfall on that cash flow for that period.
  • Shortfalls are part of the projection result: per period, per cash flow and in total.
  • The cumulative shortfall is a holding metric (SHORTFALL), so goals can use it, e.g. a MAINTAIN goal "bank account SHORTFALL = 0" (ADR-0014).

Alternatives considered

  • Letting the balance go negative and reporting when it is depleted: simpler, but it hides how badly the plan falls short.
  • Funding the shortfall automatically from other accounts (a withdrawal waterfall): powerful, but it is a strategy decision. It is left out for now and could later be added as an explicit model element.

Consequences

  • The rule applies to every debit from an account, including liability payments. A shortfall on a mortgage payment means the plan cannot afford the mortgage. For now it is reported only; there is no modelling of default or arrears.
  • When several debits compete for the same account in one month, the order in which they are paid decides which ones fall short. This feeds into Q-08 (order of steps within a month).
