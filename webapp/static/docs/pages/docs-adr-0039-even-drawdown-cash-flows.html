ADR-0039: Even drawdown of a pot over the remaining lifetime

  • Status: Accepted
  • Date: 2026-11-05

Context

In retirement, spending is funded from several pots (RRSP, investments that hold the sale proceeds). Fixed withdrawals from each pot cause false shortfalls when one pot empties before another. A top-up waterfall was considered. The user chose a simpler rule: spread the money available at retirement evenly over the time the person has left to live.

Decision

  • A DrawdownCashFlow draws from one account, starting at a given period, until the horizon end (the estimated death).
  • Its monthly amount is fixed at its start: balance of the account at the start ÷ number of months remaining until the horizon end.
  • Each pot gets its own drawdown. All pots are spread over the same remaining period, so they run out together, and there are no false shortfalls.
  • Retirement income = guaranteed income (e.g. pension) + the sum of drawdowns. Goals test it with a PERIOD TagSum tally (ADR-0037).

Alternatives considered

  • A top-up rule (waterfall over ordered sources): more realistic but more complex. Kept as a possible later refinement.
  • Recomputing the amount every month (balance ÷ remaining months): follows returns, but income varies month by month.

Consequences

  • Returns earned after the start are not part of the fixed amount, so a small balance remains at the end. That makes the estimate slightly conservative.
  • The amount is nominal and fixed, so its real value falls with inflation. Goals in real dollars will show the decline over time.
  • Withdrawals from tax-deferred pots are taxed as usual (ADR-0035).

Amendment (2026-11-09): annuity at the expected return, recomputed yearly (C-P3)

The Consequences above were wrong: at 5% over 25 years the "small balance" left is about 1.43× the starting balance, and income was understated by about 40%. The user reopened the decision and chose an annuity.

  • The monthly amount is the annuity payment that brings the balance to zero at the horizon end, using the account's expected returnRate (monthly equivalent r) over the n months remaining: amount = B × r / (1 − (1 + r)^−n); if r = 0, B / n.
  • It is recomputed on each anniversary of its start (ADR-0013 yearly step) from the actual balance and the months remaining, so returns that differ from expectation are spread over the remaining years. The amount is fixed within each year.
  • Optional indexed flag: the stream rises with the scenario's inflation at each anniversary (a growing annuity), giving a flat real income. The first payment is lower. This is the one withdrawal method where indexing is allowed (C-M16 rejects indexing on percent withdrawals and fixed draws only).
  • The final payment clears the remaining balance; nothing is left at the horizon end.
  • Expected return for a TAXABLE account ignores the tax on its income yield (ADR-0054); the yearly recompute absorbs the difference.

Consequences (replace the first bullet above)

  • The pot is used up exactly at the horizon end, under the expected return.
  • Unindexed: nominal income is flat within each year and changes only with returns; its real value still falls. Indexed: real income is about flat.

Amendment (2026-11-11): two independent settings; even retired

The user split the drawdown into two independent settings on a withdrawal:

  • How much: a given amount (method: amount, runs until the account is empty) or derived so the account is spent down by the end of the draw (method: annuity, the annuity of the amendment above).
  • Shape: indexing (none | inflation | custom). With indexing the payment rises each anniversary to keep its purchasing power; without it the dollars stay level. Allowed on amount and annuity; still rejected on percent (C-M16).

Banking precedents: given + level = a systematic withdrawal plan (SWP); given + indexed = the "4% rule" (Bengen 1994); derived + level = a term-certain annuity; derived + indexed = an indexed term-certain annuity, recomputed yearly as in the "annually recalculated virtual annuity" (Waring & Siegel 2015).

  • even is retired. Older files with method: even are read as an unindexed annuity (the app converts them on load; the engine accepts even as an alias). Results change: even divided the balance by the months left and ignored returns.
  • The indexed flag of the amendment above is the existing indexing field.
  • Defaults taken from the proposal: the account's own annualReturn; recompute on each anniversary of the draw's first month; the first payment is set from the current balance in the first month with a balance (after same-month transfers), not the opening balance; ordinary (end-of-month) annuity, matching the engine's return on the opening balance.
  • The final month withdraws the whole balance. That month's return is credited after it, so about one month's return stays in the account ("nothing left" becomes "about one month's return").
