ADR-0066: Timeline view of a scenario

  • Status: Proposed (experiment on branch poc/sm-triggers, with ADR-0065)
  • Date: 2026-11-13
  • Relates to: ADR-0065 (triggers), ADR-0064 (enabled), D-E1 (modifiers at their host)

Context

The builder lists items as a table. With triggers (ADR-0065) the dependencies between items (a property tax that lives with its house, a salary that ends at retirement) are separate rows; the owner asked for a picture of the scenario over time.

Decision

  • A Timeline tab draws the scenario as an SVG built by the app (webapp/timeline.py, no JavaScript library), on a time axis of the horizon, one month accurate:
    • each lasting item (recurring flow, asset, mortgage, account) is a lane from its first to its last month, clipped to the horizon;
    • git-graph style, a lane branches off its parent's lane where it starts and merges back where it ends: the parent is its host (D-E1: a mortgage's property, a contribution's account, a flow's adjustments, ...), else the lasting item whose date starts it by a trigger (e.g. property tax with the house), else the household trunk;
    • one-off items (sale, renewal, adjustment, retirement, ...) are dots on their parent's lane, else on the trunk; each retirement also gets a vertical guide;
    • triggers are dashed amber links (branch/merge) or arrows from the event's month to the start or end they set;
    • items switched off are drawn greyed at the dates they would have.
  • Dates come from the scenario with its triggers resolved (the dates the engine uses); a mortgage ends at its last movement in the run (payoff or sale); a modifier lane stops with its host. Names and dots link to the item's row in the builder.

Amendment (2026-11-13): vertical graph, like git log --graph

  • The Timeline tab opens on a vertical graph (timeline.graph): one row per happening (a lane's start or end, a one-off item) in time order, not to scale; each lasting item is a column that branches off its parent's column and merges back, columns are reused once free (as git does); each row has a line of text: the month, the item, its type, and "⚡ <trigger>" when a trigger sets that date. Within a month: one-off items, then ends (children before their parent), then starts (parents first).
  • "Oldest first" is the default (a plan reads forward); "newest first" (git's order) and the horizontal time axis (to scale) are a click away (?order=newest, ?view=axis).
  • One row per month (amended later the same day): the month's nodes sit side by side on one row; after the month, its happenings are comma-separated: one-off items, then "ends:", then "starts:" (each name links to the builder, has its tip, and "⚡" when a trigger sets that date); a long month wraps onto more lines under the same date. A column freed by an end is reused from the next month.
  • Every point, line and curve has a mouse-over tip saying what it stands for (a lane: the item, its type, months and the item it belongs to; a curve: the start or end and its trigger; a point: the start, end or one-off item and its trigger; "switched off" when off). Thin lines have an invisible 10px-wide hover area.

Alternatives considered

  • Mermaid gitGraph: branch/merge look, but no time scale (commits evenly spaced).
  • Mermaid gantt: time scale, but no arrows or branches.
  • A JavaScript charting library: interactive, but a new dependency for a PoC.

Consequences

  • A read-only view; no input is changed. Hover shows dates, click finds the item.
  • Many items starting at retirement give a bundle of arrows on its guide line.
