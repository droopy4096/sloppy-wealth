ADR-0048: Contributions to tax-deferred accounts are deducted in the same month

  • Status: Accepted
  • Date: 2026-11-07
  • Relates to: ADR-0017 (flat rate), ADR-0020 (taxable income), ADR-0035 (tax-deferred withdrawals), ADR-0018 (tax step)

Context

RRSP contributions reduce taxable income. The model taxed withdrawals from tax-deferred accounts (ADR-0035) but gave no deduction for contributions, so a $500/month contribution cost $500 of cash instead of about $350 at a 30% rate.

Decision

  • Money moved into a TAX_DEFERRED account from outside it (a contribution cash flow or one-off transfer) is a deduction in the period it is made. Only the member's own contributions count; see the amendment on employer contributions below.
  • In the tax step (ADR-0018 step 7) the period's taxable income is reduced by the deductions: tax = rate × max(0, taxable income − deductions). The deduction cannot make the tax negative; any excess is lost (no carry-forward).
  • Same-month treatment matches payroll and group-RRSP deductions at source. A yearly refund the following April would give the same yearly total a few months later, at the cost of carrying state across years.
  • Contribution room is not checked (Q-06, watch item).

Alternatives considered

  • Yearly refund in April: more realistic for self-directed contributions, but needs per-year accumulation and a dated refund event.
  • No deduction: overstates the cost of saving in an RRSP.

Consequences

  • Transfers between tax-deferred accounts (e.g. RRSP to RRSP) are not deductions.
  • With the income-less mode (ADR-0044) there is no taxable income, so deductions have no effect (see Q-29).

Amendment (2026-11-07): employer contributions are not deductions

  • An employer contribution is taxable to the employee and deductible, so the two cancel out. It is therefore neither taxable income nor a deduction in the model.
  • Only the member's own contributions are deducted.

Amendment (2026-11-08): pension plan contributions are deductions too

  • A member's contribution to a defined-benefit pension plan (ADR-0052 amendment) is a deduction under the same rule as a contribution to a TAX_DEFERRED account.

Amendment (2026-11-09): refund credit when income is entered after tax (C-P2)

  • In modelled-income mode, a deduction that finds no taxable income to reduce is not lost. The excess becomes a refund credit: credit = rate × max(0, deductions − taxable income), paid into the operating account in the same tax step. Equivalently, tax = rate × (taxable income − deductions) may be negative.
  • Reason: income entered after tax (the JSON default, ADR-0020) had tax withheld at source; the contribution earns that tax back. Without the credit, a $500/month RRSP contribution at 30% lost about $1,800 a year.
  • In income-less mode (ADR-0044) the excess stays lost: no tax was withheld.
  • The ledger books the credit as a tax movement from outside to cash, sourced to the contribution(s).

Consequences

  • The "any excess is lost" rule above now applies only to income-less mode.
  • Simplification: the model assumes withheld tax at the flat rate covers the credit; it does not check that the after-tax income was large enough to have withheld it.

Amendment (2026-11-09, later): carry-forward pool replaces the refund credit (C-P2 revisited)

Supersedes the refund-credit amendment above. An uncapped credit paid cash for tax that was never withheld: a $300k RRSP transfer in a month with $2,000 of pension income would have paid about $60k.

  • A deduction the period's taxable income cannot absorb goes into a carried-forward pool (undeducted contributions, as CRA allows without time limit).
  • Each tax step applies the period's deductions plus the pool against taxable income from any source, down to 0: tax = rate × max(0, taxable − deductions − pool). What is not used stays in the pool. Tax is never negative and nothing is paid out.
  • The pool also works in income-less mode (ADR-0044): withdrawals' taxable amounts use it. Anything left at the horizon end is lost.
  • Consequence: salary entered after tax gets the benefit of its contributions only later, against taxable retirement income, not as yearly refunds. Authoring defaults salaries to before-tax (ADR-0020), so this mainly affects hand-written JSON.

Amendment (2026-11-11): deductions and the pool are per person (ADR-0061)

An RRSP contribution reduces only the taxable income of the person whose room it uses: the account's owner, or its contributor for a spousal RRSP. Each person has their own carried-forward pool; unused deductions never move between persons (Canada has no joint filing).
