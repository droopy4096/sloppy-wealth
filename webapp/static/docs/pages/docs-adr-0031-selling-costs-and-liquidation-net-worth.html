ADR-0031: Selling costs, and a liquidation-adjusted net worth

  • Status: Accepted
  • Date: 2026-11-04

Context

Net worth counts a home at its full value, but turning it into cash costs about 4–6% (realtor, legal). Comparing an owner with a renter on unadjusted net worth favours buying.

Decision

  • Asset gains sellingCostRate (a percentage of the sale price).
  • On a SaleEvent, selling costs = price × sellingCostRate. They are deducted from the proceeds and reduce the capital gain (ADR-0017).
  • A liquidation net worth is available as a tally (NetWorthTally.liquidationAdjusted, ADR-0027). It is net worth minus what selling everything in that period would cost: selling costs and capital gains tax on owned assets, and tax at the effective rate on tax-deferred account balances.

Consequences

  • Comparisons and goals can use either net worth or liquidation net worth. Rent vs buy should use liquidation net worth.

Amendment (2026-11-09): liquidation tax rate (C-P6)

  • Scenario setting liquidationTaxRate (rate series; default: the effective tax rate; listed as an applied default). It is used for every tax in liquidation net worth: tax-deferred balances, unrealised gains on TAXABLE accounts (ADR-0054) and capital gains on owned assets.
  • The pension value (ADR-0052 amendment 2026-11-09) keeps the effective rate: it is received over years, not cashed in at once.
  • Reason: liquidating everything puts it all in one tax year; the flat rate understates that tax by six figures on large balances.
