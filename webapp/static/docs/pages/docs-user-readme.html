Getting started

Sloppy Wealth is a ballpark calculator for a household's big financial questions: buy or lease, rent or buy, when a house breaks even, when you can retire. You describe your household as a set of items (incomes, expenses, accounts, properties, mortgages, vehicles, pensions) and the app plays them forward month by month over the years you choose.

Run it

You need Python 3. From a copy of the repository:

cd webapp
python3 -m venv .venv && .venv/bin/pip install -r requirements.txt   # once
.venv/bin/python app.py

Then open http://127.0.0.1:5000 in your browser.

Your data stays with you

The scenario you build lives in your browser tab while you work. Save (in the top menu) downloads it as a JSON file, which you can Load again later, compare with another, or share. The app keeps nothing: there are no accounts and no database. The tab's copy is gone once the tab is closed, so save before you close it; the app warns you about unsaved changes.

Building a scenario

  • Settings. Open the Scenario card (under Household) to set the name, the horizon (first and last month), the opening cash, tax rates and inflation, and whose dollars the "today's dollars" figures are in.
  • Adding. Each category and card has + Add buttons for the items that belong there: a vehicle's insurance is added on the vehicle's card, so it is tied to it.
  • Icons on a card. + shows what can be added to it, the green pencil edits it, the red bin deletes it (with everything tied to it, after asking).
  • Switching on and off. The checkmark at the left of a card or row switches the item off, and everything tied to it follows. Switched-off items stay in the scenario, so you can compare setups by switching them back on: no need to delete anything.
  • Estimates. Cards show a greyed figure per month, a quick estimate from the item's own amounts. Run gives the exact figures.

Calculators

The purple chart icon opens a calculator for that item:

  • Property: break-even and rent equivalent. Each month, what a sale would bring (after selling costs, the mortgage and its early-closure penalty) against everything paid for the property, and the rent that would have cost the same.
  • Vehicle: cost of ownership. Each month, everything paid for the vehicle so far, less its value, per month owned.

Good to know

  • It is a ballpark: as good as the figures you enter. Amounts are rounded to cents and the model is simple on purpose.
  • It understands Canadian and US accounts and taxes (RRSP, TFSA, RESP; 401(k), IRA, HSA). Other countries may lack features they need.
