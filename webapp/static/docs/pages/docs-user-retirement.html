When can I retire

When can I retire with an income of $NNN in today's dollars?

This tutorial models one person who wants to retire on $4,500 a month in today's dollars, living on an RRSP and the Canada Pension Plan (CPP). It finds the earliest retirement date at which the money lasts until age 90. It takes about 20 minutes. All figures are illustrative: replace them with your own.

To start from the finished scenario, download retirement.json, open Load in the app and upload it, then jump to Find the earliest date.

How the question is set up

  • The income you want is your spending: living expenses of $4,500 a month in today's dollars, rising with inflation every year, before and after retirement.
  • Before retiring, a salary pays the expenses and RRSP contributions; what is left accumulates as cash.
  • From retirement, the salary and the contributions stop. The RRSP is drawn down so that it lasts until the end, and CPP pays from age 60, with increases at 65 and 70.
  • You can retire at a date if cash never runs out before age 90, the end of the horizon.

The person in the example was born in June 1975: 60 in 2035-06, 65 in 2040-06, 70 in 2045-06 and 90 in 2065-06.

What you need

  • Sloppy Wealth running (see Getting started).
  • Your salary, spending, RRSP balance and contributions, and your CPP estimates (your Service Canada statement gives them).

1. Start the scenario

Click New, choose Canada and click Start. In the Scenario card (under Household) set:

  • Horizon from 2027-01-01 to 2065-06-01 (until age 90).
  • Opening cash 40000.
  • Income tax 28 (%) while working and Retirement tax 15 (%) after retiring: the app switches from one to the other at the retirement date.
  • Inflation 2.5 (%), Show real values in dollars of 2027-01-01.

2. Working life

Under your person (You):

  • Income: Salary, 120000 per year from 2027-01-01, taxable, indexed to inflation. No end date: retirement will end it.
  • Accounts: an account RRSP, type RRSP, opening balance 400000, return 5 (%). On its card, a contribution of 1500 per month from 2027-01-01, indexed to inflation (contributions are deducted from taxable income).

Under Household, Expenses: Living expenses, 4500 per month from 2027-01-01, amountIn "today's dollars", indexed to inflation. This is the retirement income you are aiming for.

3. Retirement

The retirement date

On your person's card, click retirement under + Add and give a first guess: 2037-01-01 (at 61½).

What stops at retirement

A trigger ties an item's start or end to another item's date. On the salary's card, click trigger: event Retirement, action end (its last month is the month before retirement). For the RRSP contributions, which sit as a row on the RRSP's card, open Builder (flat) and add a trigger under Triggers: event Retirement, target RRSP contributions, action end.

Moving the retirement date later moves both ends with it.

Drawing the RRSP down

On the RRSP's card, add a withdrawal, method annuity, indexed to inflation, and leave its start date blank: a withdrawal without a start date begins at retirement. An annuity spreads the balance over the months left, rising with inflation, so the RRSP lasts exactly until the end of the horizon. Withdrawals are taxed (at the retirement rate).

CPP

CPP pays from age 60 and, in this tutorial, increases at 65 and 70. In Pensions, add three pensions, each taxable, indexed to inflation, with amounts in today's dollars:

So CPP is $700 a month at 60, $1,100 at 65 and $1,500 at 70, in today's dollars.

Your person's section now holds the whole plan: the retirement and its triggers, the salary, the RRSP with its contributions and drawdown, and CPP.

4. Run it

Click Run and look at the Cash column.

Retiring in 2037-01, cash turns negative in 2051-09 (at 76) and ends $268,963 short: not enough. (Investments holds the RRSP and the value of the CPP pensions; the RRSP is spread to the end on purpose, so it is the cash that tells whether the spending is covered.)

5. Find the earliest date

Move the retirement date later until cash stays positive to the end. On your person's card, click the green pencil on the Retirement row, change the date and click Save; then Run again.

So with these figures you can retire in August 2038, at 63, on $4,500 a month in today's dollars.

In the first month of retirement, CPP pays $918.46 and the RRSP $4,387.52, less $795.90 of tax, against $5,904.39 of living expenses (the $4,500 grown by twelve years of inflation). The cash saved while working covers the difference until the CPP increases at 65 and 70.

6. What moves the date

Change one thing at a time and run again:

  • Spending. A lower target is a strong lever: edit the living expenses.
  • Saving more while working: a larger RRSP contribution, or a TFSA (an account of type TFSA, with a withdrawal at retirement like the RRSP's).
  • CPP timing. Starting CPP later pays more each month; change the pensions' start dates and amounts.
  • Returns. The RRSP's 5% return matters a lot over 40 years; try 4% or 6%.

Make it closer to your situation

  • A spouse. Add a second person (under Household) with their own income, accounts, CPP and retirement date; items then belong to one person or the other.
  • Spending that changes in retirement. An adjustment on the living expenses gives them a new amount from a date.
  • OAS (Old Age Security, from 65) is another pension.
  • Save your work with Save in the menu; the browser tab forgets it once closed.

The answer is a ballpark: as good as the figures you enter, returns and inflation above all.
