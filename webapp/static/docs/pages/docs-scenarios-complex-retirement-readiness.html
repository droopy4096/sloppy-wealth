Scenario: retirement readiness after selling one property

Question

"How many years from today until I can sell one property and retire on $3,500/month?" Deliberately simple: only the important points are modelled (ADR-0040).

Given

FactValue
Income$150,000/yr (crude: net income + one "monthly expenses" figure, or just "free income")
Property Avalue $600k, mortgage ~$300k
Property Bvalue $850k, mortgage ~$400k
Vehicles2 × value ~$12k, maintenance $1,500/yr each
RRSP$80k today, contributions $600/month
Guaranteed pension$1,500/month, starts at retirement
Target retirement income$3,500/month
Horizon endestimated death (a date, or years from today); no age needed

Answering "how many years"

A variant sweep (ADR-0038): shift everything tagged exit and the end of everything tagged work by +0, +1, …, +20 years. The engine runs the set in lockstep, and the earliest variant whose goal holds is the answer.

Ignored on purpose

Vehicle replacement (V1), RRSP contribution tax deduction (T1), renting, age.

Inputs (from the user)

⚠ Remaining amortisationnot given; 25 years assumed below

Goal (decided)

The $3.5k must also cover B's mortgage, which continues in retirement. So the goal is on a derived tally, disposable retirement income:

  • disposable = TagSum(retirement-income, IN, PERIOD) − TagSum(mortgage-B, OUT, PERIOD)
  • MAINTAIN: disposable ≥ $3,500, REAL, activeFrom = activeUntil = the retirement month (ADR-0014 amendment, ADR-0027, ADR-0037). With inflation at its default of 0, real = nominal.

Free income (decided)

$25,000/yr is after mortgage payments. It is modelled with netOf = [mortgage A, mortgage B] (ADR-0042), so the engine does not count the mortgages twice. ADR-0042 is deferred (2026-11-07): until it is revisited, this scenario has to be entered as gross income and expenses instead of a single free-income figure. ⚠ Is it also after vehicle costs and RRSP contributions?

Sweep (decided)

exit shifted with BOTH, work shifted with END, +0 … +20 years (ADR-0041).

Rough hand check (illustrative only; validates the model, not a result)

Assumptions: 25-year amortisation (confirmed); free income is after mortgages, vehicle costs and RRSP contributions; neutral defaults for everything else (no tax, no selling costs, 0 returns, 0 inflation); accumulated cash is drawn down too.

  • Mortgage payments: monthly rate (1 + 0.045/2)^(1/6) − 1 ≈ 0.3715%; A ≈ $1,660.42/month, B ≈ $2,213.89/month.
  • Needed: drawdown ≥ $3,500 − $1,500 pension + $2,213.89 B mortgage ≈ $4,214/month.

So, roughly 10 years under these assumptions (4 years if B's mortgage did not have to be covered).

Side observation: B's mortgage ends in year 25. From then on, disposable income rises by about $2,200/month, so the fixed drawdown is front-loaded against a need that falls. A drawdown recomputed when B's mortgage ends would allow an earlier retirement.
