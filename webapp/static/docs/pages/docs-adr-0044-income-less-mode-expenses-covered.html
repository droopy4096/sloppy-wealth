ADR-0044: Income-less mode, where expenses are assumed covered

  • Status: Accepted
  • Date: 2026-11-07
  • Relates to: ADR-0040 (minimal input), ADR-0042 (free income net of modelled payments)

Context

Many questions are about what the holdings do (appreciation, amortisation, equity, sales), not about the household budget. Entering income and every expense just to keep cash from drifting is a burden, and an incomplete budget makes cash, and so net worth, misleading: leftover income piles up, or missing income drives cash negative.

Decision

  • A scenario has an income mode: modelled (the default, as before) or expensesCovered.
  • In expensesCovered:
    • Income cash flows are ignored, and so is income tax.
    • Recurring outgoings are assumed paid from outside the model, leaving no free money: scheduled loan payments, extra monthly loan payments, expenses and costs. They still take effect on the holdings (loans amortise, prepayments shorten them) but do not move the operating account.
    • One-off events still go through the operating account: purchases, loan advances, lump-sum prepayments, sales with their selling costs and loan payoffs.
  • Net worth in this mode means "what the holdings and the one-off decisions did", with the household budget factored out.

Alternatives considered

  • Enter a "free income" exactly equal to the expenses (ADR-0042, netOf): needs the payment amounts by hand or a netting rule, and goes stale at renewals and prepayments.
  • Let cash go negative and ignore it: hides real shortfalls among the artefacts.

Consequences

  • Shortfall capping (ADR-0015) only applies to one-off events in this mode.
  • Comparisons between a scenario in this mode and one with modelled income are not like-for-like; a comparison view should show each scenario's income mode (as it does inflation, ADR-0028 amendment).
  • Treating lump sums as paid from the operating account (not covered) is a choice made while building the PoC: a lump sum is a one-off decision funded from savings.

Amendment (2026-11-07): the source of a lump sum is chosen per lump sum

  • Each lump-sum prepayment says where its money comes from: the operating account (default) or outside the model (e.g. a gift or a bonus that is not modelled). Outside money reduces the loan without moving the operating account.
  • This applies in both income modes, and replaces the PoC choice noted under Consequences.

Amendment (2026-11-07): contributions and withdrawals of accounts

  • Contributions into accounts (RRSP, TFSA, investments) are recurring outgoings and are covered from outside: the money lands in the account; the operating account does not move.
  • Withdrawals from accounts are moves between holdings, not income: the account falls and the operating account rises by the same amount.
  • Taxes on holdings still apply: withdrawal tax (ADR-0035) and capital gains tax (ADR-0017). Only tax on ignored income disappears.
  • With no taxable income, the contribution deduction (ADR-0048) has nothing to reduce, so it has no effect in this mode. Withdrawals are taxed but contributions are not deducted; comparisons between this mode and modelled income should keep that in mind.

Amendment (2026-11-08): only income from outside the holdings is ignored (Q-32)

  • The mode ignores income cash flows from outside the model's holdings (salaries and other income items): the outside budget replaces them.
  • Guaranteed pension payments still happen. A pension is a holding (ADR-0052), so its payments are moves from the pension's value into the operating account, like account withdrawals (amendment above), and are taxed as such.
  • Net worth stays consistent: after the retirement date the pension's value turns into cash month by month instead of disappearing.
