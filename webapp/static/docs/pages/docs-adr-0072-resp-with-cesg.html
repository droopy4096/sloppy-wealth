ADR-0072: RESP accounts with the Canada Education Savings Grant (Canada)

  • Status: Proposed (owner's decision 2026-11-18, Q-48)
  • Date: 2026-11-18
  • Relates to: ADR-0011 (investment accounts), ADR-0060 (contribution room), ADR-0071 (jurisdictions: Canada's package)

Context

Canadian households save for children's education in a Registered Education Savings Plan (RESP). The owner's scenario held its RESP as a TFSA-like account, which leaves out the federal grant, a sizeable part of an RESP's growth. The RESP rules (CRA, ESDC): contributions are not deductible; growth is sheltered; on withdrawal the contributions come back tax-free and the growth and grants are taxed to the student (usually nil). The Canada Education Savings Grant (CESG) adds 20% of contributions, at most $500 of grant a year, or $1,000 when unused grant room from earlier years is caught up, and $7,200 over the beneficiary's lifetime. Lifetime contributions are limited to $50,000 per beneficiary.

Decision (Q-48, owner 2026-11-18: "RESP + CESG grant")

  1. Canada gets an account type resp: contributions not deductible, growth sheltered, withdrawals untaxed (the student's tax, usually nil, is not modelled).
  2. One RESP account per beneficiary; a family plan is entered as one account per child.
  3. CESG: every contribution or one-off transfer into an RESP brings a grant paid into the account from outside (movement GRANT), 20% of the amount, limited by:
    • the grant room: $500 for each calendar year from the horizon's first year, plus the unused room at the start (field unusedGrantRoom, default 0);
    • at most $1,000 of grant in a calendar year;
    • $7,200 over the account's life, less the grant already received before the horizon (field grantReceived, default 0).
  4. Lifetime contribution limit $50,000 per account, less the contributions made before the horizon (field contributedSoFar, default 0): a contribution or transfer beyond it is capped, the excess stays in cash (as with contribution room, ADR-0060). An opening balance from outside is the existing balance: no grant, no limit.
  5. The employer match does not apply to an RESP in practice; if entered, it gets no grant.

Alternatives considered

  • RESP as an account type only (TFSA-like, no grant or limit): the scenario's current workaround, without the grant.
  • Full rules (withdrawals split into returned contributions and educational assistance payments taxed to the student, grant only until the beneficiary is 17, grant repayment if the child does not study): much more to enter and to test; deferred.

Consequences

  • Account items take type-specific fields (unusedGrantRoom, grantReceived, contributedSoFar) declared by the account type (AccountType.fields, ADR-0071).
  • Canada's sample (conformance, ADR-0071) gains an RESP, so its golden result changes.
  • Implemented 2026-11-18 on poc/sm-resp: jurisdictions/ca/resp.py. The grant is paid in Canada.on_contribute (which now gets the movement's source); the lifetime cap is in Canada's limits (RoomLimits.cap). A yearly amount is paid monthly, so its grant is 20% of each month's share (e.g. 12 x 83.33 = 999.96 for $5,000 a year).
  • Not modelled: the Canada Learning Bond, provincial grants, additional CESG for lower-income families, the grant age limit, grant repayment, and tax on educational assistance payments.
