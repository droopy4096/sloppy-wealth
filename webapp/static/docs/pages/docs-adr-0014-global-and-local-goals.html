ADR-0014: Global and local goals

  • Status: Accepted
  • Date: 2026-11-04

Context

Goals are observed during a projection, and the engine reports when they are met (ADR-0006). Some goals concern the whole economic unit, while others concern a single holding. They also differ in whether today's dollars or nominal dollars make sense.

Decision

  • Global goal: evaluated against the economic unit as a whole, based on net worth. Typically expressed in real (today's) dollars, e.g. "net worth ≥ $2M in today's dollars".
  • Local goal: attached to one specific holding (an account, asset or liability). It states which condition that holding should reach. Typically nominal, e.g. "mortgage paid off", "interest paid off", or "house value exceeds the amount invested into the house".
  • Every goal declares its value basis (nominal or real). The basis has a default per goal type: real for global goals, nominal for local goals.

Consequences

  • Local goals are conditions over a holding's metrics, not just a threshold on its balance. Holdings must expose metrics such as value, balance, cumulative amount invested and cumulative interest paid.
  • Global goals need net worth in each period, in both nominal and real dollars.

Amendment (2026-11-04): simplifications

  • The value basis is fixed by goal type: global goals are always real, local goals are always nominal, and there is no override. This is the simplest option to implement; overrides can be added later if needed.
  • Local conditions can compare metric to metric as well as metric to threshold. Example: "house value exceeds amount invested", where amount invested = purchase price + costs paid on the asset (the asset's cost basis, accumulated over time).
  • The "interest paid off" example is dropped.

Amendment (2026-11-04): goal kinds

  • Every goal has a kind:
    • REACH: met the first time its condition becomes true. The engine reports the period it is first met, or "not met within horizon".
    • MAINTAIN: the condition must hold for the whole horizon. The engine reports the first period it is violated, or "held throughout".
  • Example: "the retirement fund lasts until age 80" is a MAINTAIN goal, e.g. no shortfall on the bank account (see ADR-0015).

Amendment (2026-11-05): basis of goals that are neither global nor local

  • Goals on other tallies (TagSum, Derived; ADR-0027) are neither global nor local presets, so they declare their basis explicitly (NOMINAL or REAL). Global and local goals keep their fixed basis.
