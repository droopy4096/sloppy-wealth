ADR-0060: RRSP and TFSA contribution room limits

  • Status: Accepted
  • Date: 2026-11-11
  • Relates to: ADR-0002 (single economic unit), ADR-0048 (RRSP contribution deduction), ADR-0030 (sweeps), ADR-0025 (one-off transfers), ADR-0052 (pensions), ADR-0013 (indexing)
  • Resolves: Q-06 (contribution room part)

Context

Contributions and transfers into RRSP and TFSA accounts are unlimited today (ADR-0048, ADR-0030 watch notes). An optimisation run on a real-life scenario (2026-11-11) put several hundred thousand dollars into the RRSP within a few years, which real contribution room does not allow, and every dollar was also deducted from taxable income. Results that rely on such transfers overstate retirement income.

Canadian rules (to be parameterised, not hard-coded):

  • RRSP: new room each January 1 = min(18% of the previous year's earned income, the year's dollar maximum) − pension adjustment; unused room carries forward.
  • TFSA: new room each January 1 = the year's annual limit; unused room carries forward; withdrawals are added back on the following January 1.

Decision

  1. Over the room: cap. A contribution or a transfer into an RRSP or TFSA moves only the amount within the available room; the excess stays in cash. The capped amount is shown (ledger amount and the event's log detail). Only the amount actually contributed is an RRSP deduction (ADR-0048).
  2. Inputs: a contributionRoom item per account type (accountType: rrsp | tfsa; at most one per type). It holds the current room and its as-of date, and the yearly rules: RRSP rate (18%) and yearly dollar maximum; TFSA annual limit; with their indexing. All accounts of that type share the room. The builder shows it as its own form. Without the item, that type has no limit (older files unchanged).
  3. Earned income: an earnedIncome flag on income items, default = the item's taxable (salaries count automatically). Pensions (their own item type) and account withdrawals never count. RRSP room for year Y uses the earned income paid in Y − 1 (gross, as credited by the engine).
  4. Pension adjustment: not modelled for now. The current-room input (from the CRA notice of assessment) already reflects past pension adjustments. Revisit when a modelled job has a workplace pension plan.
  5. Yearly maximums are indexed with the existing indexing field (none | inflation | custom, ADR-0013): the item states the maximum and the year it applies to; later years grow by the indexing rate from that year. Rounded as CRA does: the TFSA limit to the nearest $500, the RRSP dollar maximum to the nearest $10. A custom rate can stand in for wage growth (the RRSP maximum follows the average wage).
  6. Every account of type rrsp is treated fully as an RRSP, including the owner's "RRSP+401k": room applies to every contribution and transfer into it; no exemption for 401(k) rollovers (model a rollover as part of the opening balance).

Mechanics (defaults following the CRA rules; not separate owner decisions)

  • What uses room: contributions (the own part first, then the employer match, both within the room; a match beyond the room is not paid), one-off transfers into the account. Opening balances (at the horizon start, from outside or moved from cash) and returns do not (amended 2026-11-18, below).
  • Timeline: the room given is the room available in the month of its as-of date. Movements before that month are not checked. Each following January 1 adds the new room (RRSP: min(rate × the previous calendar year's earned income, the year's maximum); TFSA: the year's annual limit). Earned income before the horizon start is not known and counts as 0.
  • TFSA withdrawals are added back to the room on the following January 1. RRSP withdrawals are not.
  • Not modelled: the age-71 RRSP deadline (C-P10), the $2,000 over-contribution buffer, the first-60-days rule, spousal plans.
  • Visible in results: each January 1 room update and each capped movement is an event in the event log with the room before and after; the remaining room per type is reported monthly.

Alternatives considered

  • Cap and send the excess to a fallback account: rejected for now (simpler; the owner can add a separate contribution to another account).
  • Contribute in full and charge the CRA over-contribution penalty (1% a month above the $2,000 buffer): rejected, nobody plans for a penalty.
  • Validation error: rejected; the room depends on income at run time.

Consequences

  • Plans stay feasible and realistic; money over the room remains in cash (0% return) unless the owner directs it elsewhere.

Amendment (2026-11-11): room per person (ADR-0061)

A contributionRoom item has an owner; at most one per (person, account type). Room is used by the owner's accounts, or by the contributor of a spousal RRSP. RRSP room grows from that person's own earned income; TFSA withdrawals add back to the owner's room. "Spousal plans" are no longer in the not-modelled list. The room columns in the results show the household total per type.

Amendment 2026-11-18: an opening balance is current state, not a contribution

Owner's decision (Q-51, found while writing the developer guide): an account opened with its balance moved from cash (openingFrom: cash) was capped by the room and used it up, though it was neither deducted nor granted. A user enters the current state of an account (its balance) and of the room separately, so an opening balance, from cash or from outside, is not a contribution: moved in full, never capped, uses no room, no deduction, no grant (RESP, ADR-0072). Money put in after the opening is a contribution or transfer as before. Alternative: keep capping openings from cash (rejected: it would require inflating the room to make the current state match).
