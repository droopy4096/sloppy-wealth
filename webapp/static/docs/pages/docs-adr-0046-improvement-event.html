ADR-0046: Improvements (construction, renovation) are events on an existing asset

  • Status: Accepted
  • Date: 2026-11-07
  • Relates to: ADR-0025 (financed purchase, market value), ADR-0017 (cost basis), ADR-0022 (variable costs), ADR-0034 (sale settles secured loans)

Context

Building a house on a parcel already owned, or renovating, adds cost and value to an asset that exists. Modelling it as a second asset (the PoC's separate "house" asset on a lot) splits one property in two: two sales to keep in step, a secured loan attached to only one part, and per-property settings (value rate, selling cost, principal residence) duplicated.

Decision

  • A new event, ImprovementEvent, on an existing Asset:
    • date, cost : Money, paidFrom : Account, financedBy : Loan [0..1] (as for a purchase, ADR-0025: the loan advance funds the cost);
    • valueAdded : Money [0..1], defaulting to cost.
  • Effects in its period (events step, ADR-0018):
    • cash (and/or the loan advance) pays the cost;
    • the asset's cost basis and acquisition price increase by the cost (capital gains, ADR-0017; variable costs, ADR-0022);
    • the asset's value increases by valueAdded, then keeps growing at its value rate.
  • A loan financing the improvement is normally secured by the same asset, so a sale of the asset settles it (ADR-0034).

Alternatives considered

  • Keep one asset per construction or renovation: no model change, but the problems above.
  • Purchase followed by a Revaluation: does not raise the cost basis, so capital gains would be wrong.

Consequences

  • The baseline scenario's lot and house become one property: the parcel purchase plus an improvement (its cost financed by a mortgage, a value added above the cost).
  • A value added above the cost is an unrealised gain in net worth, as with a purchase market value (ADR-0025 amendment).
  • Maintenance set as a % of acquisition price rises after an improvement, since a larger house costs more to maintain.
