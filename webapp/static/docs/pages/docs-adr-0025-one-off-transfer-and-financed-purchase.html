ADR-0025: Generic one-off transfers, and purchases financed by a loan

  • Status: Accepted
  • Date: 2026-11-04

Context

Lease down payments, one-off bills and gifts are single movements of money that are not tied to a loan. A financed purchase is paid partly from an account and partly by a loan.

Decision

  • OneOffTransfer event: amount, from : Account [0..1], to : Holding [0..1], tags. Leaving out from means money comes in from outside (e.g. a gift). Leaving out to means money leaves the economic unit (e.g. a bill, a down payment).
  • PurchaseEvent gains financedBy : Loan [0..1]. The loan's principal covers part of the price; the rest comes from paidFrom.

Consequences

  • OneOffTransfer debits follow the capping and shortfall rule (ADR-0015). They run in the events step (ADR-0018 step 4), after income.
  • LumpSumPrepayment stays as the specialised event for prepaying a loan, because it is subject to prepayment options.

Amendment (2026-11-07): a purchase may set a market value different from its price

  • A PurchaseEvent has an optional marketValue. When given:
    • cash leaves paidFrom (and the financing loan) for the price;
    • the asset's cost basis and acquisition price are the price (capital gains, ADR-0017; variable costs, ADR-0022);
    • the asset's value starts at marketValue and is then projected by its value rate.
  • The difference is an unrealised gain (or loss) that shows in net worth from the purchase period. On a later sale of an asset that is not a principal residence, it is part of the taxable gain.
  • Typical use: a house built for less than it is worth (e.g. built for $300,000, market value $450,000). Equivalent to a purchase followed by a Revaluation (ADR-0033) in the same period, without needing two events or an ordering rule between them.

Amendment (2026-11-09): closing costs are inside the mortgage amount (C-P12)

  • Assumption: purchase closing costs (land transfer tax, legal fees, inspection) are rolled into the mortgage; the mortgage principal the user enters already includes them. There is no closingCosts field.
  • Closing costs therefore do not raise the cost basis. A capital gain on a later non-principal-residence sale is overstated by them (a few percent of the price).
  • If they were paid in cash instead, the user adds them to the down payment or as a one-off transfer in the purchase month.
