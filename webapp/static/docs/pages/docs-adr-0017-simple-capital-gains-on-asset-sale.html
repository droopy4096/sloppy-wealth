ADR-0017: A simple capital gains rule on asset sales

  • Status: Accepted
  • Date: 2026-11-04

Context

Selling an asset that has appreciated, such as a second property, is taxable in Canada, and leaving that out overstates the proceeds. ADR-0004 covered only the tax treatment of accounts.

Decision

  • The tax-rules interface (ADR-0004) gains a rule for asset sales:
    • capital gain = sale price − cost basis
    • taxable gain = capital gain × inclusion rate (from the jurisdiction's rules)
    • tax = taxable gain × the scenario's effective tax rate (an assumption, as a rate series)
  • RealEstate has a principal residence flag. A sale of a principal residence is exempt.
  • A capital loss produces no tax and no credit (simplification).

Consequences

  • Scenarios need an effective tax rate assumption. It is also the natural rate for other simple tax effects (taxable account income, RRSP withdrawals). See Q-09.
  • Timing simplification: the tax is deducted at the sale, from the proceeds, instead of at the next tax filing.
  • Only one property should be marked as principal residence at a time. This is a validation rule, not enforced by the engine.

Amendment (2026-11-04): one flat rate

  • A single effective tax rate (rate series) applies to every taxable effect in the simple tax model: taxable capital gains, income earned in taxable accounts, and withdrawals from tax-deferred accounts. There are no separate rates per effect.

Amendment (2026-11-09): taxable-account income

  • "Income earned in taxable accounts" now means the account's income yield only. Capital growth in a TAXABLE account is deferred and taxed at the inclusion rate when withdrawn (ADR-0054).

Amendment (2026-11-09): per-sale tax rate (C-P6)

  • SaleEvent gains an optional taxRate: the rate applied to that sale's taxable gain instead of the effective rate. Default: the scenario's effective rate. Same pattern as the per-account withdrawal rate (ADR-0035).
  • Reason: a large one-off gain lands in the top brackets (about 53.5% in Ontario); the flat rate understates it.

Amendment (2026-11-09): partial exemption by designated years (C-P13)

  • The principal-residence flag is the initial designation only. The designation moves with ResidenceSwitchEvent (ADR-0056); a sale is exempt in the share (1 + designated years) ÷ owned years, capped at 1.

Amendment (2026-11-11): tax per person (ADR-0061)

Taxable income is accumulated per person, each with their own flat rate (person's effectiveTaxRate / retirementTaxRate, default the scenario's). Investment income of an account is taxed to the account's owner. Gains on household assets (properties, vehicles) use the sale's taxRate or the scenario rate and are charged to the first person (assets have no owner). Still one flat rate per person, no brackets.
