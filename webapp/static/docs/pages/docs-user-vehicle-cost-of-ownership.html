Vehicle cost of ownership

What does my car really cost me per month, financing included, and how much of it is fuel?

A car costs far more than its loan payment: the value it loses, the loan's interest, insurance, maintenance and fuel. This short tutorial models one car bought with a five-year loan and reads what owning it costs per month, first with fuel, then without. It takes about 10 minutes. All figures are illustrative: replace them with your own.

To start from the finished scenario, download vehicle-cost-of-ownership.json, open Load in the app and upload it, then jump to Read the cost.

What you need

  • Sloppy Wealth running (see Getting started).
  • The car's price and your down payment, the loan (rate and length), and the yearly running costs.

1. Start the scenario

Click New, choose your jurisdiction and click Start. In the Scenario card (under Household) set the horizon from 2027-01-01 to 2034-12-01 (eight years, how long you plan to keep the car), Opening cash 20000, Income tax 0 and Inflation 2.5 (%).

Under your person's Income category, add Take-home pay of 4000 per month from 2027-01-01, not taxable, indexed to inflation, so that cash can pay for the car. Income doesn't count in the car's cost.

2. The car and its loan

The car

In the Vehicles category, click vehicle next to + Add: name Car, purchase date 2027-01-01, price 38000, losing 15 (%) of its value a year.

The loan

Open the car's card and click mortgage under + Add: in the app, any loan is a "mortgage", and one added on a vehicle's card finances that vehicle. Enter the amount borrowed, 32000 (the price less a $6,000 down payment), the rate 6.9 (%) and amortization 5 years. Leave the start date blank: the loan starts with the purchase.

The money borrowed goes into cash, which pays the full price; the loan's payments then follow monthly ($630.67 here), and its interest counts as a cost of the car.

The running costs

On the car's card, click associatedExpense and add:

NameAmountPerIndexing
Insurance1800yearinflation
Maintenance700yearinflation
Fuel250monthinflation

The car's card now shows the loan and the three costs, with this month's estimates.

3. Read the cost per month

Click the purple chart icon on the car's card. For every month, the Vehicle cost of ownership page shows:

  • Value: what the car is worth.
  • Loan interest: the interest paid on its loan so far.
  • Paid so far: the price, plus the interest and every running cost so far.
  • Cost/mo: (paid so far − value) ÷ months owned, what owning the car has cost per month.

With fuel, the car costs $927.59 a month over the five years of the loan, and $847.84 a month over the eight years: keeping a car longer spreads its loss of value and the loan's interest ($5,839.91 in all) over more months.

The cost per month starts low ($458.33 in the first month: one month of running costs, and a car still worth its price) and climbs as the value falls.

4. The same car without fuel

Fuel depends on how much you drive, so it helps to see the cost without it. On the car's card, untick the checkmark on the Fuel row. It turns grey and leaves the calculation, but stays in the scenario.

Open the cost of ownership page again:

With fuelWithout fuel
Cost per month over 5 years$927.59$664.77
Cost per month over 8 years$847.84$574.84

So fuel is about $273 a month of this car's cost, and the car itself (value lost, interest, insurance and maintenance) about $575. Tick the checkmark again to bring fuel back. Any item can be switched the same way: try the insurance, or the loan (the car then is paid in cash, without interest).

Make it closer to your situation

  • Maintenance that grows with age: see Lease or buy a vehicle for steps by age.
  • Selling the car. Add a sale on its card at the date you would sell: it pays off what is left of the loan, and the table ends the month before.
  • Other costs. Registration, tires or parking are more associated expenses; a one-off repair is an associated cost on a date.
  • Save your work with Save in the menu; the browser tab forgets it once closed.

The answer is a ballpark: as good as the figures you enter.
