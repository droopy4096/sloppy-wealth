ADR-0029: The engine derives scheduled loan payments

  • Status: Accepted
  • Date: 2026-11-04

Context

With term renewals at new rates (ADR-0019), a loan's scheduled payment changes over its life. A hand-entered payment cash flow goes wrong after the first term.

Decision

  • A loan derives its scheduled monthly payment with the standard amortisation formula, from the outstanding balance, the monthly rate (per its compounding convention) and the remaining amortisation.
  • The payment is recalculated whenever the rate changes: at each term start of a TermScheduleRate, and at each segment boundary of another rate generator. Each recalculation uses the remaining amortisation at that point.
  • The loan has paymentFrom : Account. Its scheduled payment is a derived debit in the liability-payments priority group (ADR-0018 step 5).
  • Prepayments (extra cash flows to the loan, lump sums) do not change the scheduled payment. They shorten the remaining amortisation, which is the usual Canadian practice.
  • The last payment is capped at the outstanding balance.

Consequences

  • Scenarios no longer contain payment cash flows for loans. The two-houses walkthrough is updated accordingly.
  • "Double payments from July 2027" is an extra cash flow to the loan, subject to its prepayment options.

Amendment (2026-11-07): the final payment clears the balance

  • The last scheduled payment of the amortisation is the exact amount still owed: the remaining balance plus that month's interest. It may be slightly more or less than the regular payment. This is how lenders settle the final instalment.
  • Reason: every payment is rounded to the cent (ADR-0009), so a level payment leaves a small remainder (e.g. $0.39 on a $560,000, 25-year mortgage in the PoC). Without this rule the loan would run one extra month and loan goals would be reported late.
  • The same rule applies at the end of a reset amortisation (ADR-0043). Prepayments that end the loan early are still capped at the amount owed.

Amendment (2026-11-07): when the first payment falls

  • By default the first scheduled payment falls in the month after the advance, which is the usual practice for monthly Canadian mortgages. Interest for that first month is charged in the first payment.
  • A loan may give an optional firstPaymentDate to override this (e.g. a lender that sets the first payment two months out).
  • The interest adjustment for an advance in the middle of a month is not modelled; the engine works in whole months (ADR-0007).
  • Consequence: in the month of a financed purchase no loan payment is made. Comparisons (e.g. rent vs buy) show this in their first month.

Amendment (2026-11-09): fixed-payment variable and interest-only loans not supported (C-P15)

  • Decision: not modelled for now. Every loan recalculates its payment on a rate change to keep the amortisation (amortising only).
  • Not expressible:
    • Fixed-payment variable mortgage: payment kept at rate changes, amortisation stretches, balance can grow past the trigger rate.
    • Interest-only loan / HELOC: interest paid, principal outstanding until repaid or sold.
  • Reason: the user's mortgages are fixed-rate, renewed at fixed rates.
  • Watch item: add a loan paymentMode (AMORTIZING default, FIXED_PAYMENT, INTEREST_ONLY) if a variable-rate mortgage or HELOC is planned.

Amendment (2026-11-09): interest adjustment before a late first payment (C-M6)

  • When firstPaymentDate is later than the month after the advance, the interest accrued from the advance until the month before the first payment is an interest adjustment: paid in cash from paymentFrom on the first payment date, booked as interest. It is not capitalised.
  • The balance at the first payment is the principal; the scheduled payment is computed from it over the full amortisation, which starts at the first payment.
  • Follows Canadian lender practice (interest adjustment date). Fixes the PoC balloon payment.

Amendment (2026-11-09): prepayments shorten the amortisation until the next renewal (C-M7)

  • The Decision's "They shorten the remaining amortisation" holds until the next renewal. At a renewal the remaining amortisation resets to original − elapsed (ADR-0043), so the saving returns as a lower payment, unless the renewal keeps the payment (ADR-0043 amendment 2026-11-09).
