ADR-0019: Rates come from rate generators; each holding declares its compounding

  • Status: Accepted
  • Date: 2026-11-04
  • Refines: ADR-0005 (the rate series is now one kind of rate generator)

Context

Real rate histories are more complex than one series of dated segments. A mortgage might run 2 years at one rate, renew for 5 years at another, then for 3 years at a third. Canadian fixed-rate mortgages also compound semi-annually by law, whereas accounts usually compound monthly or annually.

Decision

  • Every rate in the model is provided by a RateGenerator: a function from period to rate, rateFor(period).
  • Implementations include:
    • ConstantRate
    • SegmentedRate: dated segments, i.e. the original "rate series" of ADR-0005.
    • TermScheduleRate: an ordered sequence of terms, each with a start, a length in months and a rate. It models mortgage renewals (2y, then 5y, then 3y). It also exposes termAt(period).
  • New behaviours mean new implementations. A probabilistic generator is where Monte Carlo plugs in (the rate source seam of ADR-0005).
  • Each holding declares its compounding convention (e.g. MONTHLY, SEMI_ANNUAL, ANNUAL). The engine converts the annual rate from the generator into a monthly rate using that convention. Mortgages default to SEMI_ANNUAL.

Alternatives considered

  • A single global conversion (r/12 everywhere): wrong for Canadian mortgages.
  • Mortgage terms as a separate structure next to the rate: the term boundaries and rates would be duplicated.

Consequences

  • The mortgage's early closure penalty rule uses termAt(period) to tell whether a sale or payoff happens inside a term, and how much of the term remains.
  • Generators are time-shiftable (ADR-0007): shifting one moves all its segments or terms.
