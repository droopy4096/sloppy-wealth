ADR-0061: Second earner — persons in the household

  • Status: Accepted
  • Date: 2026-11-11
  • Relates to: ADR-0002 (single economic unit), ADR-0059 (retirement event and retirement tax rate), ADR-0060 (contribution room), ADR-0048 (RRSP deduction pool), ADR-0017 (tax)
  • Resolves (when accepted): Q-06 (per-person rules), Q-39

Context

The household has one earner. A second family income needs its own RRSP/TFSA accounts and contribution room, retirement date and tax. Canadian rules that are per individual (tax brackets, RRSP room from own earned income, TFSA room) cannot be expressed while the whole household is one taxpayer.

The household stays one economic unit for money (ADR-0002): cash, properties, mortgages, vehicles, expenses, sales and net worth remain shared. Only the rules that are per individual move to a person. Scenario files without persons behave as one implicit person using the scenario's tax rates, so their results are unchanged.

The last single-earner implementation is tagged single-earner (poc/state-machine, e12a902).

Decision

  1. A person is a person item (id, name, optional effectiveTaxRate and retirementTaxRate; blank rates use the scenario's). It appears in the builder like other items and gives the owner field a list to choose from. Rejected: a free-text owner only (nowhere for per-person rates, typos unnoticed); a list of persons in the scenario settings (outside the item list and builder forms).
  1. Items with an owner field: income, accounts (rrsp, tfsa and nonRegistered), pensions, contributionRoom and the retirement event. Contributions, withdrawals and transfers take the owner of the account they touch (no field of their own). Properties, mortgages, vehicles and expenses stay household-level. Rejected: owners on properties as well (work for a capital-gains split that is not modelled); non-registered accounts household-level (their investment income could not be taxed to one person).
  1. A blank owner means the first person (in item order). Files without persons use one implicit person. Adding a partner only needs the owner set on the partner's own items. Rejected: blank owner an error once persons exist (every existing item must be edited); a separate "household" owner with the first person's rules (same effect, less clear).
  1. Household items tied to retirement name the person: retirementOf (a person id; blank = the first person). Applies to every household item whose date can be "at retirement" (e.g. a sale, a payoff, an expense end). Owned items follow their owner's retirement. Rejected: always the first person (no sale at the partner's retirement); household-wide first/last retirement (no per-item control, surprising when dates change).
  1. Tax rates per person: optional effectiveTaxRate and retirementTaxRate on the person; blank = the scenario's rate. Each person's taxable income is taxed at their own rate, switching to the retired rate at their own retirement (ADR-0059). Rejected: one household rate with only the switch per person (cannot show different brackets); per-person brackets (large new feature, against flat rates of ADR-0017).
  1. The carried-forward deduction pool (ADR-0048) is per person. An RRSP deduction reduces only its owner's taxable income; the unused part carries forward in that person's pool. Canada has no joint filing and RRSP deductions are not transferable between spouses. Rejected: one household pool (overstates savings when incomes or rates differ). The legal route to a similar effect is a spousal RRSP (decision 7).
  1. Spousal RRSP is built now: an rrsp account may name a contributor (a person id, different from its owner; blank = the owner). Contributions, transfers and openings from cash into it use the contributor's RRSP room and reduce the contributor's taxable income (contributor's deduction pool). Withdrawals are taxed to the owner. Not modelled at first: the attribution rule (withdrawals within about three calendar years of a contribution taxed to the contributor). Rejected: deferring it.
  1. Pension income splitting: deferred (watch item). Pension income is taxed to its owner only; results overstate tax when one partner holds the pension. Possible later form: a splitToSpouse fraction (0–50%) on pensions. Rejected for now: the fixed fraction and an automatic best split each year.
  1. One owner per account. RRSPs and TFSAs are individual by law; a joint non-registered account is assigned to one person, whose rate taxes its investment income. A 50/50 split is approximated by entering two accounts. Rejected: an ownerShare split on non-registered accounts (a split in every tax step).
  1. Results stay household-level. Monthly rows keep their columns: cash, investments, net worth, total tax. The room columns show the household total per type (the sum over persons; unchanged with one person). Per-person figures (tax, deductions, room movements) are visible in the tax and ledger pages and the event log. The plan is judged on household outcomes. Rejected: per-person room and tax columns; per-person investments and net worth.

Mechanics (defaults; not separate owner decisions)

  • Implicit person: a file without person items has one person (id self) with the scenario's rates; results are identical to before (golden suite unchanged).
  • Owner inheritance: contributions, withdrawals, transfers and drawdowns take the owner of their account; an employer match goes with its contribution.
  • Retirement: one retirement event per person (ADR-0059); a salary ending at retirement uses its owner's date; every consumer of the retirement month asks for a named person's retirement.
  • Tax: one tax accumulator per person: own taxable income, own rate (switching at own retirement), own deduction pool; one TAX movement per person per month. Investment income of an account is taxed to its owner.
  • Room: contribution room is per (person, type): a contributionRoom item has an owner; RRSP room grows from that person's earned income; TFSA withdrawals add back to the owner's room. A spousal RRSP uses its contributor's room.
  • Not modelled: pension income splitting (decision 8), the spousal RRSP attribution rule (decision 7), joint ownership splits (decision 9), CPP/OAS per person (ADR-0055 unchanged), transfer of credits between spouses.

Consequences

  • A household with two earners gets realistic room, deductions and tax per person while every money outcome stays household-level (ADR-0002 holds for money).
  • Existing scenario files and the golden suite are unchanged.
  • About eleven engine parts read the retirement month; all must go through one lookup by person, with a test each, or they silently use the first person's date.
  • Results understate available savings options (no pension splitting) and overstate tax when one partner holds most retirement income.
  • The last single-earner implementation is tagged single-earner.
