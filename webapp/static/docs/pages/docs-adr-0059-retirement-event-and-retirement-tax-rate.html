ADR-0059: A RetirementEvent triggers everything tied to retirement, including the tax rate

  • Status: Accepted
  • Date: 2026-11-09
  • Relates to: ADR-0052 (retirement date, pensions), ADR-0017 (effective tax rate), ADR-0035 (tax-deferred withdrawals), ADR-0039/0049 (withdrawals), ADR-0038 (sweeps), ADR-0007 (events)
  • Amends: ADR-0052 (Scenario.retirementDate; accounts not tied to retirement)

Context

In the user's baseline, a defined-benefit pension after retirement was taxed at the working-years rate (30%) instead of a lower retirement rate (about 20%). One flat rate for a whole life overstates tax in retirement. Retirement also switches many things at once (salaries end, pensions and draws start, some expenses start), and the user wants one item that triggers them all.

Decision

  • New event RetirementEvent (date). At most one per scenario. Its month is the retirement month. It replaces Scenario.retirementDate; files that still carry retirementDate are read as an implicit RetirementEvent (not both: validation error).
  • From the retirement month:
    • Salaries and other cash flows with endsAtRetirement stop (the month before); cash flows with startsAtRetirement start (ADR-0052 amendments, unchanged).
    • Pensions without their own start date start (unchanged).
    • Withdrawals from accounts (retirement funds) without their own start date start. A withdrawal with a start date keeps it. (Changes ADR-0052's "accounts are not tied to it".)
    • The tax rate switches from effectiveTaxRate to retirementTaxRate.
  • Scenario.retirementTaxRate (rate; optional; default: effectiveTaxRate, i.e. no switch; listed as an applied default). It applies to all taxable income and deductions from the retirement month: pensions, salaries still running, non-registered income, and tax-deferred withdrawals without their own withdrawalTaxRate (an account's own rate still wins, ADR-0035). It is a two-segment form of the effectiveTaxRate series (ADR-0017).
  • Shifting or sweeping the RetirementEvent moves all of the above together (ADR-0038).

Alternatives considered

  • A tax rate per pension (like withdrawalTaxRate): fixes the pension only, not other income after retirement.
  • Keep retirementDate as a scenario field: works, but the user wants retirement as a visible event among the items, shiftable like other events.

Consequences

  • Without a RetirementEvent nothing is tied to retirement: pensions and withdrawals need their own start dates (validation error otherwise), and one tax rate applies.
  • Liquidation tax (ADR-0031) and per-sale rates (ADR-0017) are unaffected.

Amendment (2026-11-09): every end date may be a date, none, or retirement

  • Every item with an end date (income, expense, contribution, withdrawal, extra mortgage payment, pension) ends in one of three ways, chosen explicitly in authoring:
    • on a date (endDate),
    • never (perpetual: runs to the horizon end or until exhausted),
    • at retirement (endsAtRetirement: stops the month before the RetirementEvent).
  • endsAtRetirement therefore extends from income, expenses and contributions to withdrawals, extra mortgage payments and pensions (e.g. a bridge benefit started early that stops at retirement).
  • JSON stays lenient for older files: if both endDate and endsAtRetirement are set, the earlier applies; without a RetirementEvent, endsAtRetirement has no effect.
  • Authoring defaults: salaries and contributions "at retirement"; everything else "never".

Amendment (2026-11-09): a sale may happen at retirement

  • A SaleEvent may set atRetirement instead of a date: it happens in the RetirementEvent's month and moves with it (e.g. downsizing when retiring). A sale has either a date or atRetirement, not both; atRetirement without a RetirementEvent is a validation error.
  • All rules that refer to the sale month (mortgage payoff, renewals before the sale, improvements and market adjustments before the sale) use the retirement month.

Amendment (2026-11-09): a lump-sum prepayment may happen at retirement and pay off the loan

  • A LumpSumPrepayment may set atRetirement instead of a date (same rules as a sale at retirement).
  • It may set payOff instead of an amount: it pays the loan's whole outstanding balance in that month (after that month's scheduled payment), so the user need not know the balance in advance. Its penalty field applies as for any lump sum (ADR-0047).

Amendment (2026-11-09): a one-off transfer may happen at retirement

  • A OneOffTransfer (ADR-0025) may set atRetirement instead of a date (same rules as a sale at retirement), e.g. moving sale proceeds into an RRSP when retiring.
  • A transfer into a tax-deferred account is a deduction as before (ADR-0048).

Amendment (2026-11-11): one retirement event per person (ADR-0061)

"At most one" becomes at most one per person; the retirement event has an owner. Owned items tied to retirement (salary end, pension start, withdrawal start) follow their owner's retirement. Household items tied to retirement (sale, payoff, transfer, expense end) name the person with retirementOf (blank = the first person). Each person's tax rate switches at their own retirement.
